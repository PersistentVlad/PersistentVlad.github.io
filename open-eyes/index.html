01 / Purpose

Connect existing capabilities
into a shared direction.

PDSN — Perimeter Distributed Surveillance Network — is a conceptual architecture for distributed observation. O.P.E.N. EYES is the public initiative for exploring it through collaboration.

The current strategy is to connect existing infrastructure and Ukrainian developers around an interoperable observation layer. The aim is to preserve accumulated engineering experience and explore joint civilian, public-safety and security applications, including international opportunities.

The next useful step is a bounded joint pilot with an agreed observation problem, participating platforms and measurable criteria.

02 / Application layers

Three contexts.
One observation foundation.

Each layer needs its own permissions, governance and operating constraints. Select a layer to explore the intended scope.

Environmental observation and early warning

Ecosystem monitoring, natural hazards, critical infrastructure and industrial safety, with humanitarian response and emergency connectivity as related application directions.

Illustrative pilot: combine fixed and mobile observations around an infrastructure site and measure how quickly a verified hazard reaches an operator.

03 / System architecture

From separate sensors
to an accountable event.

Heterogeneous observation

Integrate fixed towers, mobile platforms and tethered or airborne nodes. Sensor modalities may include optical, thermal, acoustic or radio-frequency observations, depending on the approved use case.

Shared event representation

Describe observations with time, source, confidence and provenance so multiple reports can contribute to an interpretable event.

Verification & escalation

The proposed orchestration layer evaluates corroborating and conflicting evidence, then routes uncertain or consequential events for human review.

Adaptive coverage

Represent node and link availability explicitly. When a component becomes unavailable, expose the coverage gap and coordinate appropriate observation continuity.

04 / Collaboration model

Build around an interface
and a measurable pilot.

01 / ALIGN

Choose the observation problem

Agree on one setting, the information operators need and the limits of acceptable data collection.

02 / CONNECT

Use participating platforms

Define a shared observation and event contract. Keep ownership and platform-specific implementation explicit.

03 / EVALUATE

Test continuity and usefulness

Measure event latency, corroboration, false alerts and behavior during node or link loss.

This is a proposed development sequence. No signed partners, deployed pilots or field results are claimed.

05 / What needs validation

Coverage is useful
only when the evidence is usable.

01
Interoperability

Can independently developed platforms exchange observations without losing timestamps, source identity or confidence meaning?

02
Trust and event quality

Can the system distinguish corroboration from duplicated reports and preserve contradictory evidence for review?

03
Resilience

How does awareness degrade during node or communication loss, and how clearly are gaps presented to operators?

04
Governance

Who can access each observation, how long is it retained, and which decisions require a human? These rules must be defined for each deployment context.
