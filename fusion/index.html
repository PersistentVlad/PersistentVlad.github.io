01 / Research direction

Make the architecture
explicit enough to test.

These concepts frame questions for plasma physics, control engineering and simulation. The contribution is a system architecture and a proposed validation path.

DTMC and CMIFR have separate public repositories and concept white papers. They address different abstractions: magnetic topology as an engineering object, and the coupling of consecutive ignition cycles. Both remain early-stage research.

02 / DTMC

Dynamic Topological
Magnetic Confinement.

Can magnetic topology become a programmable engineering object while remaining compatible with established electromagnetic theory and plasma physics?

DTMC proposes an architectural layer in which topological state is represented explicitly and its evolution is planned, monitored and adjusted. The design intent is to separate high-level topological objectives from a particular magnetic hardware implementation.

State & planning

Represent the magnetic configuration and desired evolution as explicit system state, then define topological objectives and control primitives.

Feedback & execution

Relate diagnostic evidence to control objectives. Hardware-specific actuators must realize the requested evolution within physical and engineering limits.

The public repository describes a conceptual architecture, introduced mathematical representation and proposed reference control architecture. Further formalization, numerical simulation and experimental validation are required.

03 / CMIFR

Cyclic Micro-Ignition
Fusion Reactor.

Can coupled fusion pulses achieve higher overall gain than an equivalent sequence of independent pulses?

The central hypothesis is a Persistent Ignition Zone (PIZ): residual plasma, magnetic structure or thermodynamic conditions might contribute to subsequent ignition cycles. The project calls this proposed inheritance “pulse-to-pulse memory.”

Conditions carried forward

Investigate which residual conditions could be useful, how long they survive and whether maintaining them costs less than reconstructing them for each pulse.

Cycle architecture

The conceptual framework includes cumulative focus injection geometry, counter-impact compression, magnetic ash exhaust and a gradient fuel packet strategy. These are research elements requiring physical validation.

The repository identifies CMIFR as an early-stage theoretical framework at TRL 1–2. The gain question remains open; no demonstrated net-energy production or operational reactor is claimed.

05 / Proposed validation path

Progress through
falsifiable stages.

01 / FORMALIZE & SIMULATE

Specify the physical model

Define state variables, boundary conditions, actuator limits and diagnostics. Identify assumptions that determine feasibility.

02 / TEST SUBSYSTEMS

Isolate critical mechanisms

Use suitable non-fusion experiments to investigate formation, control, stability and the persistence of relevant conditions.

03 / INTEGRATE

Evaluate the complete cycle

Only after earlier evidence supports proceeding, assess integration at an appropriate research facility with a full energy and loss accounting.

A proposed sequence, not a funded or scheduled experimental programme.

06 / Collaboration

Connect systems thinking
with specialist validation.

I contribute architectural decomposition, state and cycle modeling, interface definitions and staged evaluation planning. Specialist work in plasma physics, magnetics, diagnostics, power systems and numerical simulation is essential to assess the concepts.

Useful next contributions include a feasibility review, identification of a decisive simulation and selection of one subsystem experiment with clear success and failure criteria.
